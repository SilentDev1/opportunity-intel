# Subscription economics scenarios

These are sensitivity scenarios, not forecasts. The only reviewed opportunity volume is the
16-actionable bounded corpus; it is not yet a monthly run rate. Therefore cost-per-opportunity is
shown only at hypothetical relevant monthly volumes.

| Relevant opportunities/month | $49 | $79 | $99 | $149 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | $12.25 | $19.75 | $24.75 | $37.25 |
| 8 | $6.13 | $9.88 | $12.38 | $18.63 |
| 16 | $3.06 | $4.94 | $6.19 | $9.31 |
| 24 | $2.04 | $3.29 | $4.13 | $6.21 |

Vendor simulations find matches for all 16 reviewed actionables for broad cleaning/MSP/insurance
profiles, but only five for security and four for pest control. This concentration means a single
statewide price may deliver very different value by category. Real monthly volume and vendor
ratings are required before pricing conclusions.

| Customers (scenario) | $49 MRR | $79 MRR | $99 MRR | $149 MRR |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | $490 | $790 | $990 | $1,490 |
| 50 | $2,450 | $3,950 | $4,950 | $7,450 |
| 100 | $4,900 | $7,900 | $9,900 | $14,900 |
| 500 | $24,500 | $39,500 | $49,500 | $74,500 |

These totals ignore acquisition cost, churn, support, source maintenance, and lead competition.
The same opportunity may be valuable to different noncompeting service categories. Within one
category/territory, unlimited distribution can erode value; limited competition or exclusivity
could preserve value but would constrain revenue. No allocation mechanism should be built before
vendor tests show that competition is a real objection.
